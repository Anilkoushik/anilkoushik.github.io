About

I work on data-driven transportation systems and urban mobility modeling, with a focus on activity-based travel demand models built with machine learning.

I'm a Specialist Scientist at CiSTUP, Indian Institute of Science, where I develop trip-based and activity-based travel demand models for the city of Bengaluru. My current work covers city-scale mobility demand models and methods for mobility system planning that use multimodal urban datasets. I also mentor Master's and PhD students in transportation modeling.

My PhD at IIT Delhi treated a person's daily activity schedule as a categorical time series and modeled it with LSTM networks. I then used transfer learning to move these models between cities and SHAP to explain what drives them. Before research, I taught civil engineering at PES University and worked as a design engineer on the Mumbai Monorail and Chennai Metro projects.

Research Interests

Activity-based travel demand

Modeling full-day activity schedules with LSTMs and neural networks, including ANN frameworks with correlated error structures.

Interpretable machine learning

Using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to explain neural-network mode choice and activity schedule models.

Spatial transferability

Testing and improving how well travel behavior models carry over to new cities through transfer learning.

City-scale mobility planning

Inferring land use from satellite imagery, GPS trajectories, transit ticketing and points of interest; on-demand shared taxi and auto-rickshaw services for metro access.

Choice modeling with deep generative models

Variational autoencoders with Gaussian mixtures to find latent classes in mode choice data.

Formal languages for travel

Generating activity-travel sequences with grammar and automata theory, using stochastic finite state machines.

Education

  1. 2018 – 2024

    PhD, Transportation Engineering

    Indian Institute of Technology Delhi
    Thesis Data-driven methods for activity schedule modeling Treats the activity schedule as a categorical time series modeled with LSTM networks, so every part of the day is modeled together and the time budget holds automatically.

    Modeling all dimensions of the schedule together avoids error propagation, and the time-series form gives the schedule a tabular structure. To deal with limited data, the thesis tests and improves the model's spatial transferability with transfer learning. It uses SHapley Additive exPlanations to interpret the model and see how variables influence it. It also introduces an ANN-based framework that adds correlated error terms to activity pattern models, allowing more flexible substitution patterns.

    Full thesis in the IIT Delhi repository
